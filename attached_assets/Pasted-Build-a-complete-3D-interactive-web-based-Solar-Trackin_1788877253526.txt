Build a complete **3D interactive web-based Solar Tracking Simulator** called:

# Energy-Aware Adaptive Solar Tracking System

The project should be a **3D website**, not a Python desktop application.

The main purpose is to simulate and compare different solar-tracking strategies and determine which strategy produces the highest **NET energy**, taking both solar energy generation and motor energy consumption into account.

The project should look like a polished engineering/science mini-project suitable for a college demonstration.

---

## 1. Core Concept

Create a 3D scene containing:

* A solar panel mounted on a rotatable mechanism
* The Sun
* Visible sunlight/ray representation
* Ground/platform
* Panel support structure
* Motors/rotation mechanism
* Optional simple LDR sensor representation

The Sun should move across the sky during the simulation.

The solar panel should change its orientation depending on the selected tracking algorithm.

The user should be able to visually see the panel tracking the Sun in real time.

---

# 2. Tracking Modes

Implement these five modes:

### 1. Fixed

The panel remains stationary.

### 2. Time

The panel changes its angle according to a predetermined time-based tracking schedule.

### 3. LDR

Simulate an LDR-based tracking system.

The system should compare light intensity from different directions and rotate the panel toward the stronger light source.

### 4. Dual Axis

The panel continuously attempts to align itself with the Sun using two-axis movement.

### 5. Adaptive

This is the main proposed algorithm.

The Adaptive algorithm should consider:

* Current panel angle
* Desired Sun angle
* Expected additional solar energy from moving
* Motor energy required to move
* Tracking deadband
* Current irradiance

The panel should move only when the expected energy gained from better alignment is greater than the energy required by the motor.

The objective is:

**MAXIMUM NET ENERGY**

rather than simply maximum solar energy.

---

# 3. Important Energy Calculations

Calculate three major values for every tracking mode:

### Solar Energy

Energy generated by the solar panel.

Use a simplified model such as:

Power = Panel Area × Solar Irradiance × Alignment Factor × Panel Efficiency

Where the alignment factor depends on the angle between:

* Sunlight direction
* Panel surface normal

Only useful positive alignment should contribute.

---

### Motor Energy

Calculate energy consumed by the tracking motor.

Use:

Motor Energy = Motor Power × Motor Operating Time

Accumulate motor energy throughout the simulation.

---

### Net Energy

The most important metric:

Net Energy = Solar Energy Generated − Motor Energy Consumed

Display all three values clearly.

# 10. Adaptive Algorithm Logic

Implement the Adaptive algorithm using this basic decision process:

1. Determine current Sun direction.
2. Determine current panel orientation.
3. Calculate the expected energy improvement if the panel moves.
4. Estimate the energy required by the motor to make the movement.
5. Compare expected energy gain against motor energy cost.
6. Apply the deadband.
7. If moving is worthwhile:

MOVE PANEL

8. Otherwise:

KEEP PANEL STATIONARY

The algorithm should prioritize:

**Net Energy = Solar Energy − Motor Energy**

not simply panel alignment.

# 18. Important Accuracy Requirement

This is a simulation, not a real photovoltaic physics simulator.

Clearly separate:

* Solar-energy model
* Motor-energy model
* Tracking algorithms
* Visualization
* UI

Do not present simulated energy values as real-world measurements.

Use consistent units:

* Power → W
* Energy → Wh
* Angle → degrees
* Area → m²
* Efficiency → %

# 19. Final Goal

The final website should communicate one central engineering idea:

> A solar tracker should not simply move as much as possible to follow the Sun. It should move intelligently when the additional solar energy gained is greater than the energy consumed by the tracking mechanism.

The final project should therefore compare:

**Fixed**

**Time**

**LDR**

**Dual Axis**

**Adaptive**

and determine which produces the highest **NET ENERGY** under the selected simulation conditions.

Build the project so that all values, graphs, bars, calculations, animations, and comparisons are generated from the same underlying simulation engine and remain synchronized when the user changes settings.
